When a relationship ends, one of the first concerns for many parents is how their children’s financial needs will continue to be met. It is a common, and important question: “What does child support actually cover?”



At Hazel Family Law & Mediation, we know these conversations are rarely just about money. They are about care, stability, and making sure children continue to thrive after family change.



# **Understanding child support**



In Australia, both parents, whether married, de facto, or never in a relationship, share the legal responsibility to support their children.



Child support is designed to ensure that children have what they need to live comfortably and securely, no matter how their family structure has changed.



Services Australia manages most child support assessments. When an application is made, they use a standard formula that considers:



* The number and ages of the children.
* Each parent’s income.
* How much care each parent provides.



The assessment aims to share the financial responsibility fairly between parents, according to capacity and care arrangements.



# **What child support is meant to Cover**



Child support is intended to contribute to a child’s everyday needs, the essentials that allow them to live a safe and balanced life.



This usually includes:



* Food and nutrition.
* Housing and utilities.
* Clothing and personal care.
* Basic medical and dental expenses.
* Ordinary education costs.



These payments can also contribute to general household costs, such as rent, car expenses, or bills because these form part of the environment that supports a child’s wellbeing, even if not spent directly on the child.



# **Schooling, health and activities**



Education costs like school uniforms, excursions and basic school fees are generally covered within regular child support payments.



Where children have additional educational, medical needs or higher healthcare costs, parents can ask Services Australia to review their assessment. Adjustments may be made if it is shown that these expenses are ongoing and significant.



Activities such as sport, music or other extracurricular pursuits are treated differently. These may be covered when both parents agree or where they’re included in a child support agreement. Often, these are called *“non-periodic payments”*; contributions for specific additional costs beyond the basics.



# **What child support does not usually cover**



Certain expenses sit outside what is considered a child’s normal living costs. These can include:



* Private school tuition.
* Private health insurance.
* Certain special needs or therapies.
* Optional extracurricular activities not agreed to by both parents.



Where these kinds of costs arise, it is best for parents to have clear agreements in place, ideally in writing, so expectations are understood and the child’s needs remain at the centre of any decision.



# **When payments fall behind**



Sometimes, child support is not paid as it should be. If that happens, Services Australia has powers to recover the amount owing through various enforcement actions.



If a parent is more than 30 days late, they may face formal recovery action or have payments intercepted through wages or tax refunds. The purpose is not to punish but to ensure children continue to receive the financial support they need.



# **Can child support be changed?**



Yes. Circumstances change and child support can change with them.



You can apply for a reassessment if, for example:



* Your income has changed.
* You have lost employment or become unable to work.
* Your care arrangements have changed.
* The other parent’s circumstances have changed.



Keeping records and communicating early helps ensure the system remains fair for everyone.



Services Australia also undertakes re-assessments when parties file their tax returns.



# **When does child support end?**



Child support usually continues until a child turns 18 or finishes secondary school, whichever is later. 



In some cases, support can extend further, particularly where a child has special needs or is completing further study. Those adult child maintenance claims are not done by Services Australia.



# **If you think the assessment is not fair**



If you believe your assessment does not reflect your situation, you can ask for a review through Services Australia. Common reasons include:



* Incorrect income calculations.
* Errors in care percentage.
* Special or changed circumstances.



It is important to seek advice early as small corrections can make a significant difference over time.



# **Privately negotiating child support**



While Services Australia manages most child support assessments, some parents prefer to negotiate private child support arrangements as part of their broader property or parenting settlement. This approach can offer greater flexibility, allowing parents to design arrangements that reflect their real-life circumstances, such as how school fees, medical costs or extracurricular activities will be shared.



A private agreement can take the form of a limited or binding child support agreement, depending on how formal and long-term you wish it to be. These agreements can include both regular payments and specific contributions (for example, school or health expenses), provided they are agreed by both parents.
