Separation does not always mean leaving the family home. For some couples, living under the same roof while ending their relationship is a practical solution, particularly when children, housing constraints or finances are involved. However, living together does not mean you are not legally separated. Understanding how the law defines and recognises “separation under one roof” is crucial for protecting your rights, navigating property settlements, child support, government benefits and meeting the requirements for divorce.



Hazel Family Law & Mediation’s approach is grounded in empowering individuals with knowledge so that even in complex or unconventional situations, people can make informed confident decisions.



# **What is separation under one roof?**



Separation under one roof occurs when a couple has ended their relationship but continues to live together. Australian family law recognises this as a valid form of separation if there is evidence that both partners are living separate lives under one roof, including:



* Genuine breakdown of the relationship: Emotional and physical intimacy has ceased, and the partners no longer behave as a couple. Acknowledging this breakdown openly is crucial for clarity and mental wellbeing.
* Intent to live separate lives: Both parties demonstrate independent routines, social lives and future plans, showing their separation is intentional and lasting.
* Independent finances: Separate bank accounts, spending and contributions to household costs highlight financial independence and reduce the risk of disputes.
* Distinct daily routines: Separate bedrooms, meal arrangements and limited shared activities make the living situation sustainable while clearly defining separation.



In some situations, one partner may be the primary earner, making it difficult or impractical to split finances. This is completely acceptable. Couples can continue managing finances as they were during the relationship while still maintaining other aspects of separation, such as independent routines and boundaries.



It is important to note that this list is not exhaustive. Every family’s circumstances are unique, and other factors may also indicate that a couple is living separate lives under one roof. Even if you do not meet the above criteria, you may still be considered legally separated. Courts take a holistic approach, considering the overall nature of the relationship, daily routines, financial arrangements and intentions of both parties. The key is demonstrating that, despite sharing a home, each partner is living a separate life and the relationship has genuinely broken down.



# **Divorce and the 12 month separation requirement**



Even while living together, you can apply for divorce after a 12 month separation period. The court considers:



* Intentions to live separate lives: Courts will examine whether each person has genuinely moved on from the relationship emotionally and practically.
* Physical and emotional separation: Evidence like sleeping arrangements, distinct routines and reduced intimacy is important.
* Public acknowledgment of separation: Friends, family or colleagues noticing the couple is no longer together strengthens the case.



## **Reconciliation considerations:**



* A reconciliation lasting less than three months can still count toward the 12 month separation period.
* If a reconciliation exceeds three months, the separation period typically restarts. Documenting periods of separation and any short reconciliations prevents confusion and ensures smooth legal processes.

**Child support and parenting arrangements**



Every family will manage child support and parenting arrangements differently while living together. There is no single “right” way to do it. Some parents may choose to keep finances entirely separate, others may maintain shared expenses; some may rotate who spends time with the children in a structured schedule, while others may adopt a more flexible approach. What matters most is that the arrangements work for your family, prioritise the wellbeing of the children, and allow both parents to maintain clear roles and responsibilities. We advise focusing on cooperation, consistency and reducing conflict, so children feel secure and supported during this transitional period.



# **Property settlement and financial Implications**



Property settlements can be complex under one roof. However, even while cohabiting, finalising property settlements can provide clarity and empower individuals to move forward with confidence.



# **Government benefits and centrelink**



Every family manages finances differently, especially while living separately under one roof. Some couples may continue sharing certain expenses, while others may maintain or move to full financial independence. When it comes to government benefits or Centrelink, it is important to be aware that your separation status can affect eligibility and payment amounts. Accurate reporting and providing proof of separation may be required to ensure you receive the correct support. We encourage families to consider these practical implications while recognising that there is no one-size-fits-all approach. What matters most is finding a financial arrangement that works for your unique situation while remaining compliant with government requirements.



# **Family violence and safety considerations**



At Hazel Family Law & Mediation, we prioritise the safety and wellbeing of all clients, and it is critical to acknowledge that living separately under one roof is not appropriate if there is a risk of family violence. Family violence can include physical harm, emotional abuse, coercion, intimidation or controlling behaviour. Even if separation under one roof is possible in theory, it must never compromise the safety of you or your children.



If there is a history, ongoing risk or potential for family violence, alternative living arrangements should be explored immediately. This may include staying with family or friends, accessing safe housing services or involving legal authorities and support agencies. Safety should always come before financial, legal or logistical considerations.



We believe protecting vulnerable members of the family is paramount. No financial arrangement, co-parenting schedule or government benefit is worth risking physical or emotional harm. Even when living separately under one roof, establishing clear boundaries, documenting arrangements and seeking support should only ever occur within a safe environment.



# **Conclusion**



Understanding the legal aspects of separation under one roof equips individuals to protect their rights, navigate child support and property settlements and meet divorce requirements. Our approach ensures that legal clarity is paired with practical empowerment, helping people make informed, confident decisions even in complex circumstances.
