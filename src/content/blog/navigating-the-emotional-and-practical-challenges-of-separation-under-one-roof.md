Separation is emotionally challenging; living under the same roof with your ex-partner can intensify stress. However, for some couples, this arrangement offers financial, emotional and practical benefits. Our aim is to empower you with practical strategies, emotional support and clear steps, to help you navigate the transition safely and confidently while prioritising wellbeing and children’s stability.



# **Benefits of living separated under one roof**



* Reduced financial pressure; sharing a home allows you and your former partner to avoid the cost of maintaining two households, freeing up funds for essentials or future plans. This financial breathing room can prevent additional stress during separation.
* Stability for children; children benefit from a familiar environment, consistent routines and minimal disruption in school, friendships and extracurricular activities.
* Time to adjust emotionally; gradual transition allows you and your former partner to adapt to single life without the immediate upheaval of moving out.
* Financial planning opportunities; living together can provide space to save, divide expenses and prepare for future living arrangements.
* Avoids moving stress; maintaining the current residence can reduce logistical challenges and give you and your former partner time to plan your next steps.



# **Creating healthy boundaries**



Clear boundaries are essential when living separately under one roof. They help prevent conflict, reduce stress and support emotional wellbeing for everyone involved, including children. While every family’s circumstances are unique, and there is no one-size-fits-all approach, the following suggestions can provide a starting point. We encourage families to be mindful of what will and will not work for their situation, and to adapt boundaries to suit their specific needs.



## **Suggested boundaries can include:**



* **Personal space:** designate private areas such as bedrooms or workspaces, and respect each other’s need for solitude. Physical separation helps reinforce the idea that each person is living an independent life, even under the same roof.
* **Communication:** keep discussions focused on practical matters, for example, household responsibilities, finances, and the children, while avoiding debates about past relationship issues. Clear, respectful communication reduces tension and prevents misunderstandings.
* **Household responsibilities:** agree on who is responsible for chores, cooking, cleaning and other shared duties. A structured approach prevents resentment and ensures the household runs smoothly.
* **Meal arrangements:** separate meal times or spaces can help establish routine and independence. Sharing meals is optional but it should never create emotional conflict.
* **Parenting roles:** set clear routines for childcare, school drop-offs and extracurricular activities. Discuss responsibilities openly, support each other’s decisions and aim to minimise conflict for the sake of the children.
* **Financial boundaries:** maintain separate bank accounts where possible, clarify who is paying for household costs and document financial arrangements. Even if full financial independence is not feasible, transparency is key to reducing disputes.
* **Social boundaries:** Agree on how you will engage with friends, new partners or extended family while living together. Respect for privacy and autonomy is essential to prevent discomfort or misunderstandings.



Boundaries should be **flexible, clearly communicated and mutually respected**. What works for one family may not work for another, and adjustments may be necessary as circumstances evolve. The goal is not to create distance for the sake of distance but to establish a safe, manageable and emotionally healthy environment for everyone in the household.



# **Co-parenting while living together**



Even when separated under one roof, children’s wellbeing should remain the central focus. You and your former partner should aim to create a stable, supportive environment for children while navigating the complexities of living together separately. Every family is different, so there is no single “right” way to co-parent in these circumstances. What matters is that arrangements work for your household and protect your children emotionally and physically.



## **Guiding principles for co-parenting under one roof:**



* **Keep discussions child-focused:** ensure that conversations in front of children are centred on their needs, routines and wellbeing. Avoid discussing past relationship issues or conflicts in their presence. Do not talk about negotiations about property settlement or parenting arrangements, discussions or negotiations with lawyers or Court proceedings. That can create anxiety or force the children to take sides.
* **Maintain consistent routines:** children thrive on predictability. Keep schedules for meals, school, homework, extracurricular activities and bedtimes consistent. That will reassure you children that, despite changes in their parents’ relationship, their daily life remains stable.
* **Support each other’s parenting decisions:** agree on key parenting choices, such as discipline, education or health matters, and avoid undermining one another in front of the children. Presenting a united approach helps children feel secure and reduces confusion.
* **Minimise conflict:** conflict between parents can be emotionally harmful. Try to focus on calm and constructive interactions, even when disagreements arise. Using neutral spaces for difficult conversations or considering mediation can help keep tensions low.
* **Manage child related expenses collaboratively:** clearly outline how costs for schooling, activities, healthcare and other child-related expenses will be managed. Transparency prevents misunderstandings.
* **Seek professional support when needed:** counselling, mediation or support groups can provide guidance on co-parenting strategies and conflict resolution. Hazel Family Law & Mediation can help you with tools to communicate and work together effectively.


## **Telling children about the separation:**



* **Provide age appropriate explanations:** children need to understand the situation in a way they can process, without being burdened by adult concerns.
* **Reassure them of parental love:** emphasise that both parents continue to love and care for them, and that the separation is not their fault.
* **Present a united front:** whenever possible, discuss the separation together, showing consistency and cooperation. That helps children feel secure and reduces confusion about their family structure.



Co-parenting while living together requires patience, communication and intentionality. Families may adjust routines or approaches over time, and that is completely normal. The goal is to create a safe, nurturing environment where children feel loved, supported and protected, even as the adults navigate the complexities of separation.



# **Emotional and mental wellbeing while living separated under one roof**



Living under the same roof while separated may seem like a practical solution but it is important not to underestimate how challenging it can be. Even in amicable situations, sharing a home over a prolonged period can be emotionally taxing and tensions can rise if legal matters, such as property settlement, child support or parenting arrangements become contentious. What might start as a manageable arrangement can quickly become stressful, particularly if the separation process becomes drawn out or ends up in protracted court proceedings. Acknowledging the potential difficulties upfront allows families to plan carefully, set boundaries and seek the right support to protect their wellbeing and that of their children.



Living with a former partner while trying to navigate a separation requires conscious strategies to manage stress, prevent conflict and protect your emotional health.



**Prioritise self-care:** separation is often emotionally taxing and it is crucial to carve out time for activities that support your mental health. That might include exercise, creative outlets, meditation, journaling or simply quiet time alone. Self-care helps maintain clarity and resilience, enabling you to make thoughtful decisions rather than reacting impulsively to stress or conflict.



**Seek professional support: t**herapy, counselling or support groups can provide a safe space to process emotions, understand relationship dynamics and gain strategies for coping with living under one roof. At Hazel Family Law & Mediation we encourage clients to view professional support as an essential tool rather than a last resort. When children are involved, family counselling or mediation can also help co-parenting arrangements work more smoothly, reducing conflict and supporting a peaceful household.



**Set and respect boundaries:** clear physical, emotional and digital boundaries are fundamental to reducing emotional strain. Establishing personal spaces, private times and limits on discussions helps prevent repeated conflict and maintains mutual respect. Boundaries are not about creating distance from your children but about protecting the emotional wellbeing of all household members, including yourself.



**Use mediation or structured conflict resolution:** even with the best intentions, disagreements over daily minutiae are inevitable. Mediation can prevent escalation, clarify expectations and formalise agreements on parenting, finances or household responsibilities. 



**Document agreements for clarity and peace of mind:** whether it is parenting arrangements, financial contributions or household responsibilities, documenting agreements in writing provides clarity and reduces uncertainty. That can include informal agreements, including parenting plans, or interim court orders. We encourage documentation as a protective measure: it not only prevents misunderstandings but also provides evidence if required for legal purposes.



**Protecting yourself emotionally:** emotional wellbeing is closely linked to safety. If there is any risk of family violence, harassment or intimidation, prioritising personal safety must come first. Seeking legal advice, accessing support services and creating a safety plan are critical steps. Emotional self-care is most effective when paired with a secure environment for you and your children.



**Remember: every family is unique.**
There is no single “correct” way to maintain emotional wellbeing while living separately under one roof. We encourage families to assess what works for them, be adaptable and seek support when needed. The key is to protect your mental health, reduce conflict and create a household where children feel safe and loved, even amidst adult conflict. 



# **Conclusion**



Separation under one roof can provide financial relief, stability for children and space to adjust emotionally. Success depends on communication, boundaries, planning and professional support. 



Even in relatively amicable situations, sharing a home over weeks or months can be emotionally draining, and tensions can rise if legal matters become complicated.
