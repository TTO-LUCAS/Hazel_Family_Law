Raising children after separation involves many ongoing practical decisions and child support is often one of the most difficult to navigate. Child support is intended to help meet the everyday costs of children across two households. In practice, assessments, collection arrangements and changes in income or care can make the system feel anything but straightforward. For the parent relying on payments, uncertainty can make it difficult to budget for ordinary expenses. For the parent making payments, an assessment that no longer reflects their income or care arrangements can also create considerable stress. Clear information and accurate records matter for everyone involved.

The Australian Government has announced a significant package of proposed child support reforms, supported by approximately $182 million in funding over four years. The reforms are intended to improve collection, strengthen the accuracy of assessments and make the child support system safer where processes are being used to continue financial abuse or harassment. Mostof the proposed measures are expected to begin from 1 July 2027, subject to legislation passing. They are not yet part of the current child support system.

### Private Collect or Agency Collect?

Parents may arrange child support privately or ask Services Australia to collect and transfer payments through Agency Collect. Private Collect can work well where payments are reliable, both parents are comfortable managing the arrangement and communication remains straightforward. However, it also places responsibility on the parents to monitor what has been paid, identify missed or partial payments and keep accurate records over time.

At Hazel Family Law & Mediation, we will generally recommend Agency Collect where it is available and appropriate. There are two practical reasons for this. First, it removes one more administrative task from parents who are already managing the demands of separation and raising children across two households. Parents do not need to keep checking payments, reconciling amounts or repeatedly contacting each other about what remains outstanding. Second, Services Australia maintains the formal record of payments received and any child support debt that accrues through the Agency Collect arrangement. This can provide greater clarity if payments later become irregular or there is disagreement about what has been paid.

Agency Collect may also reduce the need for direct communication about late or missing payments. For some parents, particularly where financial discussions are a source of conflict or pressure, that distance can be valuable. Under the proposed reforms, either parent would have a clearer pathway to move from Private Collect to Agency Collect. Parents changing collection methods may also have greater scope to ask Services Australia to recover certain recently raised debts. Agency Collect cannot guarantee that every payment will be received. Collection may remain difficult where the paying parent has no identifiable income or available assets, or where the assessment relies upon outdated financial information. It may also not be necessary for every family. Some parents have managed Private Collect successfully and prefer its flexibility.

However, where parents want clear records, reliable administration and less need to manage payments between themselves, Agency Collect will often provide the stronger practical foundation.



### More child support deducted from wages

The proposed reforms are intended to increase the use of employer withholding for people using Agency Collect. For some new Agency Collect payers, and people with existing child support debt, payments would more commonly be deducted directly from wages where appropriate. Payments may also be aligned more closely with pay cycles, with the aim of making child support more regular and predictable.

Predictability matters. Children’s expenses do not pause because child support is late. Rent, food, school costs and healthcare continue regardless of whether a payment arrives on time. At the same time, the system needs to distinguish between a parent who deliberately avoids payment and a parent whose income has genuinely reduced. A person who loses employment or experiences a substantial change in income should address the assessment promptly rather than allowing an inaccurate assessment and debt to continue.



### Greater scrutiny of income information

A child support assessment generally considers each parent’s income, the number and ages of the children and the care arrangements. That formula depends upon accurate and current information. The proposed reforms include increased information sharing between Services Australia and the Australian Taxation Office, together with stronger responses to repeated failures to lodge tax returns. The reforms are intended to reduce inaccurate income estimates and identify changes in financial circumstances sooner. 

Parents should not wait for compliance action if their own income or care arrangements change. Keeping tax returns current and notifying relevant changes promptly can help reduce inaccurate assessments, accumulating debt and significant retrospective adjustments.



### Stronger safeguards against harassment and financial abuse

Child support processes can sometimes become another channel through which conflict or control continues after separation. The proposed reforms would allow the Child Support Registrar to refuse certain applications or notifications made for the purpose of harassing the other parent. They would also change how sensitive information is managed during some review and objection processes. A process is not necessarily safe simply because it is administrative. Repeated applications, unnecessary demands for personal information or threats to stop payments can place significant pressure on a parent and affect the stability available to children.

However, not every disagreement, objection or missed payment amounts to financial abuse. Some disputes arise from genuine uncertainty, changed employment, inaccurate information or the complexity of the formula. The conduct and surrounding circumstances need to be considered carefully.



### Family Tax Benefit debts

Child support reassessments can affect Family Tax Benefit Part A. A parent may be assessed as having received child support that was never actually paid and then face an unexpected Family Tax Benefit debt. The proposed reforms are intended to give some parents using Private Collect additional time to address unpaid child support, move to Agency Collect or consider whether another option is available before certain Family Tax Benefit debts are recovered.

The interaction between child support and government payments can be difficult to understand. If you receive notice of a reassessment or debt, pay careful attention to the date and obtain advice promptly about any review, objection or collection options.



### What the reforms do not resolve

The reforms focus substantially on collection, compliance, safety and income accuracy. They do not resolve every difficulty within the child support system.

Parents may still disagree about care percentages, income, school fees, health expenses, extracurricular activities and whether an administrative assessment reflects the actual costs of raising their children. Australia’s accumulated child support debt is now close to $2 billion but debt can arise for different reasons. Some non-payment is deliberate. Some reflects unemployment, reduced income, administrative delay or an assessment that has not kept pace with changed circumstances.

A system that supports children effectively must respond firmly to deliberate avoidance while remaining accurate and workable for parents experiencing genuine financial difficulty.



### What parents can do now

The proposed reforms do not mean parents need to wait until 2027 before addressing a current problem. If an assessment appears incorrect, care arrangements have changed, income information is outdated or payments have fallen behind, existing review and collection options may already be available through Services Australia.

Where payments are currently managed privately, it may also be worth considering whether Agency Collect would provide clearer records, more reliable administration and less need for direct communication. Parents may also consider obtaining advice about whether a limited or binding child support agreement could provide greater clarity about regular payments and additional expenses such as school fees, health costs or activities.
