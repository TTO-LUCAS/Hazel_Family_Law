Let’s be honest. Lawyers can be… a bit much. Pedantic, anxious, obsessed with rules and eternally focused on “the court will only do X, Y, or Z.” But before you roll your eyes and run for the hills, remember: they’re not being difficult to make your life miserable. They have a job. Their job is to make sure the system works fairly; for you, for your ex, for your kids and for the court.



So how do you deal with lawyers without losing your mind or your sense of humor? Here’s the non-lawyer survival guide.



# **1. Embrace the pedantry (kind of)**



Yes, lawyers love detail. No, you don’t have to memorize every tiny legal rule. But if you can present your facts clearly, organise your documents and answer the questions you are asked (without derailing into War & Peace territory) they will love you.



# **2. Understand the “court lens”**



Even if you’re not at court yet, your lawyer (and the mediator or FDRP) will always think about what a judge might do. This isn’t them being paranoid; it’s about making sure any parenting arrangement is in the children’s best interests and property agreement is fair and enforceable, and likely to be approved by the court. Accepting this reality will save you frustration and help you see the bigger picture.



# **3. Keep it real**



Lawyers tend to be anxious, especially about deadlines and procedural missteps. If you respond promptly, follow instructions and don’t argue about every comma in a document, you’ll win major goodwill points. Lawyers are just people too, and with a pretty stressful job.



# **4. Remember: Your lawyer is on your side but not blindly**



Think of your lawyer as your champion, not a magic wand. They genuinely want the best outcome for you but insisting on arguments the law or the court won’t back up is like trying to win a footrace in thongs, it just makes things harder. For property settlements and parenting arrangements, they’ll often refer to what a court is likely to do. It might feel a little frustrating but this approach helps create agreements that are fair, realistic and hold up over time, all while keeping the focus on what really matters: the best interests of your children and your future financial security. And yes, your lawyer secretly appreciates it when you don’t make them argue the impossible.



# **5. Bring your own perspective**



You know your life, your kids and your needs better than anyone. Give your lawyer context, practical examples and what “fair” looks like from your side. They can translate that into legal language and strategy that courts respect.



# **6. Don’t forget to breathe**



Family law is messy, emotional and often slower than you want. Humour, patience and occasional chocolate are surprisingly effective tools. Lawyers appreciate clients who can stay grounded, even when the process is frustrating. I feed my lawyer a steady diet of sugar and that seems to keep her under control.



# **7. Ask questions**



Lawyers speak a language of legalese that can feel like another planet. Don’t be afraid to ask what something means or why it matters.



# **8. Keep things organised but don’t stress about perfection**



Clear documentation makes life so much easier, financial statements, parenting plans, emails and notes but you don’t need to save (or give your lawyer) *every single thing*. What really helps your lawyer is providing information in a clear, organised way: tidy folders, labelled documents and a summary of key points. It makes them more efficient and keeps your case moving smoothly. And if you’re feeling overwhelmed or unsure how to organise it all, that’s exactly what I’m here for. I can help you get everything together in a way that’s simple, manageable and helpful for your lawyer.



# **9. Manage expectations and your frustration**



Lawyers know the law and the courts, but they don’t control outcomes. There are no guarantees and sometimes things move slower than you (and your lawyer) would like. Blaming your lawyer for what your ex, their lawyers or the court are doing rarely helps; they’re probably just as frustrated as you are. Keeping perspective and working together will make the process smoother for everyone.



# **10. Play the long game**



Family law isn’t a sprint; it’s more like a slightly frustrating marathon. The lawyers’ obsession with fairness, rules and what a court might do is there to prevent problems down the line. Trust the process, even if it moves slower than you want.



In short: understand the process, communicate clearly and keep your sense of humor. Lawyers aren’t scary monsters, they’re just pedantic, anxious humans who happen to know a lot about family law. Work with them, not against them, and you might just survive this process… maybe even with your sanity intact.



# **Fun survival tips for dealing with lawyers**



Because let’s face it — a little humor goes a long way when you’re navigating legal landmines.



# **1. Speak their language…sort of**



Throw in words like “adjournment,” “disclosure,” or “without prejudice” occasionally. Your lawyer will think you’re on top of things and you’ll secretly feel like a legal ninja.



# **2. Bring snacks to meetings**



Okay, maybe not literally but treat yourself afterward. Family law discussions can feel like mental marathons. Chocolate, coffee or your favorite snack is essential fuel.



# **3. Keep a lawyer bingo card**



Every time someone says “the court will only…,” “best interests of the child,” or “fair and equitable,” mark it down. Bonus points if you get a full line during a mediation.



# **4. Humor as a shield**



When frustration rises, a little self-deprecating joke or humorous observation can diffuse tension. Dark humour is most lawyers' secret coping mechanism. Your lawyer will appreciate it.



# **5. Celebrate small wins**



Filed a document on time? Got an answer to your question in under an hour? Survived a mediation without crying? Treat it like an achievement unlocked.



# **6. Keep a “lawyer dictionary”**



Write down the words they use and what they actually mean in plain English. It’s like learning a new language but less intimidating than the Rosetta Stone.



# **7. Embrace your inner Zen**



Sometimes the best strategy is a deep breath, a moment of perspective and the knowledge that the legal process and your lawyer is there to help you, even if they drive you slightly bonkers.



Family law can feel like a maze but it doesn’t have to be overwhelming. By staying organised, asking questions, keeping your sense of humor and working *with* your lawyer, you can get through it with your sanity, and maybe even your smile, intact.
