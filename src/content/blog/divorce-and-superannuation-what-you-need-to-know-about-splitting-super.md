Dividing property after separation or divorce can be one of the most challenging aspects of family law. In Australia, superannuation is considered a financial asset and is included in property settlements, meaning it must be considered when determining a fair division of assets.

# **Recent Changes to Superannuation Law**



In April 2022, changes to the *Family Law Act 1975* (Cth) made it easier for both parties to access superannuation information through the Australian Taxation Office (**ATO**). That change aims to increase transparency and ensure equitable outcomes.



Before these amendments, it could be difficult to obtain details about a former partner’s superannuation. Now, parties can access accurate information through the courts, helping ensure a fair split and reducing the risk of undisclosed assets.



# **How Superannuation is Split**



Superannuation splitting is separate from divorce proceedings and is usually addressed as part of property settlement negotiations. The courts can issue superannuation splitting orders, determining how superannuation is divided between the member spouse and non-member spouse.



* If both parties agree on the division, a consent order or superannuation agreement can formalise the arrangement.
* If no agreement can be reached, a court hearing may be necessary.
* In some cases, a flagging order may be used to prevent access to superannuation income streams before the split is finalised.



# **De Facto Relationships and Super**



Superannuation splitting applies not only to married couples but also to de facto couples, although the rules can vary slightly by state. In all cases, superannuation is treated as part of the property pool and is considered alongside other assets to achieve a fair outcome.

# **Requesting Information About Your Ex-Partner’s Super**



Obtaining superannuation details is a critical first step in property settlement. Courts can request information directly from the ATO through a Superannuation Information Request, usually receiving details within seven days.



Having accurate information ensures a fair and transparent property settlement, minimising the risk of hidden or undisclosed superannuation interests. Hazel Family Law can guide you through this process, helping you understand your rights and obligations.

# **Understanding Superannuation Splitting Orders and Agreements**



Superannuation splitting orders and agreements formalise how superannuation is divided. These can include provisions such as:



* Specifying the percentage split of superannuation accounts
* Establishing flagging agreements to prevent premature access to income streams
* Structuring the division of SMSF or defined benefit entitlements



Where parties cannot reach an agreement, the courts may determine the split based on contributions, financial circumstances and future needs.



# **Self-Managed Super Funds (SMSFs)**



SMSFs can complicate the superannuation division process. Unlike standard funds, both parties often act as trustees, giving them direct control over investments. Key considerations include:



* Accurately valuing SMSF assets
* Ensuring compliance with superannuation law
* Managing liquidity when assets like property or unlisted shares need to be split



Early collaboration with your SMSF accountant, financial adviser, and lawyer is essential to avoid disputes, ensure compliance and protect both parties’ interests.



# **Superannuation That Cannot Be Split**



Some superannuation interests are non-splittable under Australian law. These may include:

* Small super balances (usually under $10,000)
* Certain military or veteran pensions
* Some public sector defined benefit schemes
* Disability or compensation pensions



In these cases, courts may adjust the division of other assets to achieve fairness. Legal advice is essential to ensure equitable outcomes.
